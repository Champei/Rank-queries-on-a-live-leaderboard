# D5: Telescoping AVL split and rotation-forcing insertion

**Author:** Jay Gupta (250001035), Adversary

**Project statement (D5):** split(T, k), implemented as a sequence of joins, costs O(log n) in total because the heights telescope. Prove it, and construct an insertion order that forces rotations on a constant fraction of insertions.

**Used in:** src/structA/avl.cpp, Avl::split_node and Avl::join_nodes; adversary/generators.cpp, rotation_inserts. Sources consulted: D1 and Blelloch, Ferizovic and Sun, *Just join for parallel ordered sets* (2016).

## Rotation-forcing insertion

Order pair keys by higher score first, then lower ID. Insert (score i, ID i) for i = 0, ..., n-1. Every new key goes to the leftmost leaf. Write h(empty)=0 and bitlen(n)=floor(log2 n)+1. The following two invariants hold on the left spine after every insertion: each spine node has nonnegative balance, and each of its right subtrees is perfect. They hold for the first leaf.

At an ancestor reached by a new insertion, the left child just grew by one. Its old balance was 0 or 1, so the new balance is 1 or 2; the insertion cannot be absorbed with unchanged height. Balance 1 continues the walk and preserves both invariants. At balance 2, let x be this ancestor, y=x.left, and c=h(x.right). Before insertion h(y)=c+1. Since no lower rotation happened, y's old balance was 0, so its child heights were both c; after insertion its left child has height c+1 and its right child height c. One right rotation at x is therefore the correct branch. The new x has two perfect children of height c and thus height c+1. The new root y has two children of height c+1 and thus height c+2, x's old height. No ancestor above changes. Its right subtree is perfect, and the two spine invariants continue to hold.

If the walk reaches the root without rotation, every old spine balance was 0. Starting at the bottom, each spine node then has a perfect left subtree (induction), a perfect right subtree (the invariant), and equal child heights, so the entire previous tree was perfect. It held 2^j-1 nodes, so the next insertion number is 2^j. Conversely, only these height-growing insertions occur: a binary tree of height H has at most 2^H-1 nodes, giving H >= bitlen(n); there are only bitlen(n) powers of two at most n, giving H <= bitlen(n). Hence exactly bitlen(n) inserts grow the height and all other n-bitlen(n) inserts rotate once. For n >= 4, bitlen(n) <= 3n/4, so at least n/4 inserts rotate. The adversarial checker observed 93 rotations at n=100, 990 at n=1,000, and 9,986 at n=10,000. These observations validate the constructed sequence; the bound comes from the argument above.

## Telescoping cost of split

The internal join_nodes(L, key, R) costs at most |h(L)-h(R)|+1 (D1). Its output height is M or M+1 for M=max(h(L),h(R)): this is immediate when input heights differ by at most one. Otherwise recurse one level down the taller tree; induction gives the inner child's old height or one more, and at most one single/double rebalance restores an AVL tree of the taller input's height or one more. The mirrored case is identical. Metadata is repaired bottom-up by Avl::fix and rotations. The public Avl::join also scans boundary keys, but split_node calls the internal join_nodes and does not pay that validation cost.

Split_node follows one search path of height H=O(log n). Each node feeding the right output joins an assembled piece from below with that node and its untouched right child; the left output is symmetric. Number the right-feeding nodes from deepest to root. Let sigma_j be node j's original subtree height, rho_j its untouched right-child height, and t_j the assembled right piece after its join. Nesting gives sigma_1 < ... < sigma_m <= H and m <= H. AVL balance gives sigma_j-2 <= rho_j <= sigma_j-1.

First, h(t_j) <= sigma_j. For the deepest node, the piece t_0 is contained in its left child and has height at most sigma_1-1. Inductively h(t_(j-1)) <= sigma_(j-1) <= sigma_j-1; the join-height fact then gives h(t_j) <= max(h(t_(j-1)),rho_j)+1 <= sigma_j.

The cost of join j is at most |h(t_(j-1))-rho_j|+1. If h(t_(j-1)) <= rho_j, this is at most sigma_j-h(t_(j-1)). Otherwise rho_j >= sigma_j-2 and h(t_(j-1)) <= sigma_j-1 force h(t_(j-1))=sigma_j-1; the cost is at most 2 = sigma_j-h(t_(j-1))+1. Thus in both cases cost_j <= sigma_j-h(t_(j-1))+1.

For j >= 2, the preceding joined tree is at least as high as its right input, so h(t_(j-1)) >= rho_(j-1) >= sigma_(j-1)-2. Therefore cost_j <= sigma_j-sigma_(j-1)+3. The first join costs at most sigma_1+1. Summing telescopes:

sum(cost_j) <= sigma_1+1 + sum(j=2..m)(sigma_j-sigma_(j-1)+3) = sigma_m+1+3(m-1) <= 4H.

The left output obeys the same bound; the search path costs O(H). Therefore split costs O(H)=O(log n), rather than O(log squared n). Avl::split_score uses a pair-key sentinel between scores above and at/below the threshold, so the same argument applies. The checker audits both outputs and their rejoin at sizes up to 10,000 and on random pivots (seed 253).
